Earth Evo

The story of Earth in motion

This site introduces Earth’s transformation from its fiery beginning to the age of humans. It offers a slow, immersive journey through key stages of our planet’s evolution and the forces that made it what it is today.

Earth's History Timeline

Experience a long-form animated slideshow that moves through Earth's major stages, from planetary formation to the modern world.

01

Solar Nebula

A giant cloud of gas and dust collapsed under gravity after a nearby star exploded. The spinning disk at its center became the Sun, while material farther out began to merge into planets.

This stage set the stage for Earth’s birth, as dust particles stuck together and formed larger and larger bodies in the young solar system.

02

Planet Accretion

Earth grew when planetesimals collided and stuck together. Each impact added mass and heat, turning the young planet into a molten world with a dense iron core and a rocky mantle.

The earliest surface was a glowing ocean of magma, with constant volcanic activity reshaping the planet.

03

Moon-Forming Impact

A massive collision with a Mars-sized body blasted debris into orbit around Earth. That debris coalesced into the Moon and changed Earth’s tilt, rotation speed, and future climate.

This dramatic event also stripped away volatile material and helped set the stage for a calmer, stable planet.

04

Crust Formation

As Earth cooled, the first solid crust formed above the molten mantle. Floating volcanic islands rose, while frequent meteor strikes continued to reshape the surface.

The crust was thin and unstable, but it marked the first step toward continents and solid ground.

05

Ocean Birth

Water vapor from volcanic outgassing condensed into rain and filled low-lying basins. The first oceans formed, providing a deep, stable environment for chemistry to proceed.

Oceans also helped moderate the climate and transported minerals across the planet.

06

Organic Chemistry

Underwater vents, lightning, and sunlight sparked the first complex organic molecules. Amino acids, sugars, and other building blocks collected in pools and began forming more complex chemistry.

This prebiotic world was a laboratory for the molecules that would eventually become life.

07

First Cells

The earliest life forms were tiny cells with simple membranes and genetic material. They absorbed nutrients from the ocean and reproduced by splitting.

These first bacteria began changing the chemistry of the water and the air around them.

08

Stromatolites

Microbial mats built layered structures in shallow water. These stromatolites are among the oldest fossils on Earth and show how life began to shape its own environment.

They captured sunlight and minerals, leaving behind fossil evidence of early microbial communities.

09

Oxygen Production

Photosynthesizing microbes released oxygen as a waste product. At first, oxygen reacted with iron and other minerals, but eventually it began to accumulate in the atmosphere.

This marked the beginning of a more oxygen-rich world that could support larger and more complex life.

10

Great Oxidation Event

The atmosphere shifted dramatically as oxygen levels rose. Many earlier organisms struggled, but new forms of life evolved that relied on oxygen and released more of it.

This was one of Earth’s greatest environmental transformations.

11

Eukaryotic Cells

Larger, more complex cells with internal structures like nuclei evolved. These eukaryotes could build bigger bodies and coordinate more advanced functions.

They are the ancestors of plants, animals, fungi, and many protists.

12

Snowball Earth

At least two global ice ages covered the planet in ice. These extreme cold periods changed ocean chemistry and set the stage for later bursts of evolution.

When the ice melted, the return of warmth helped life radiate into new forms.

13

Cambrian Explosion

Life diversified suddenly in the oceans, producing many new species with hard parts, eyes, and complex body plans.

This era produced the ancestors of many modern animal groups.

14

Plants on Land

Green plants began colonizing rivers and lakes, holding soil in place and releasing oxygen into a more stable atmosphere.

These pioneering plants transformed barren land into habitable environments for later animals.

15

Forest Expansion

Forests spread across the continents, creating vast carbon-rich swamps that would later become coal deposits.

These rich ecosystems supported insects, amphibians, and early reptiles.

16

Amphibians and Reptiles

Amphibians were the first vertebrates to move onto land, followed by reptiles that could lay eggs away from water.

This evolution of terrestrial life allowed vertebrates to spread into new niches.

17

Dinosaurs Rule

Giant reptiles became the dominant land animals, filling roles from swift hunters to massive plant-eaters.

This long era saw dramatic climate shifts and the rearrangement of continents.

18

Great Extinction

An asteroid impact and volcanic activity triggered a mass extinction, ending the dinosaurs and many other species suddenly.

In the aftermath, new groups of animals had room to evolve and diversify.

19

Mammal Expansion

Mammals evolved into a wide range of shapes and sizes, exploiting forests, grasslands, lakes, and even the oceans.

The first large mammals, primates, and eventual ancestors of humans appeared during this time.

20

Hominins Walk

Early humans learned to walk upright, make tools, control fire, and live in social groups across African savannas.

These changes helped humans expand into Asia, Europe, and beyond.

21

Farming Revolution

People began planting crops and domesticating animals, which supported larger communities and led to the first cities.

Permanent settlements changed the landscape and created the conditions for writing and technology.

22

Early Civilizations

Rivers such as the Nile, Tigris, and Yellow River hosted early empires, where trade, law, and culture grew rapidly.

Architecture, astronomy, and long-distance trade began shaping a world connected by ideas and goods.

23

Modern Age

Industrialization, electricity, and digital networks transformed how humans live, work, and exchange information.

Our planet now faces new challenges of climate, biodiversity, and sustainability as we enter the Anthropocene.
